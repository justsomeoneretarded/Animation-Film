# Depressed

A 3 minute 30 second short. One visible adult protagonist.

## 1. Bedroom — night

The message had been there since yesterday. Just four words: how are you doing? He opened it, closed it, then returned to the feed. Answering should have taken a moment. Instead, he had spent another night avoiding it.

## 2. Bedroom — night

When he put the phone down, the room went quiet. He sat on the edge of the bed, still wearing yesterday's clothes. He wasn't sure what to call this feeling. Mostly, he wanted the morning to wait a little longer.

## 3. Desk — night

At the desk, he tried to write an answer on paper first. He wrote that he was tired. Then he stopped. Tired sounded like something a good night's sleep could fix. He had been sleeping badly for weeks, but that wasn't all of it.

## 4. Desk — night

He used to reply without thinking. Now he rehearsed every sentence, imagining how it might sound. Too dramatic. Too vague. Too much to put on somebody else. The longer he waited, the harder it became to explain why he had disappeared.

## 5. Desk — dawn

He turned the page and tried again. This time he wrote: I don't feel like myself. He read the words slowly. They didn't explain everything. But they were closer to the truth than the answer he usually gave.

## 6. Street — dawn

By the time he went outside, the street was beginning to wake up. He stood there with his hands at his sides, unsure where to go. He nearly returned to his room. Instead, he decided to walk as far as the park.

## 7. Street — dawn

He walked slowly. There was no sudden rush of energy, no moment when the day became easy. He watched the pavement instead of his phone. At the corner, he paused to catch his breath, then kept going toward the bench.

## 8. Park — day

Sitting down felt like admitting how much effort the short walk had taken. He rested his hands on his knees. The same question was still waiting on his phone. This time, he wondered whether it had been an invitation rather than a demand.

## 9. Park — day

He opened the message and typed a different answer. I'm not doing well. Can we talk? He read it twice. His thumb stayed above the screen while he considered deleting it. Then, before he could rewrite it into a lie, he pressed send.

## 10. Park — day

There was no immediate reply. He waited, listening to the wind move through the trees. For once, he left the phone alone. Sending the message hadn't made him feel better yet. It had simply made it possible for someone to know.

## 11. Desk — day

Back at the desk, he kept the page with the honest sentence. Beneath it, he wrote: call the clinic. He would need help finding words there, too. That could be part of the appointment. He didn't have to arrive with everything already understood.

## 12. Desk — day

The room looked much the same when he sat down again. The message was still sent. The appointment still needed making. There was plenty he couldn't manage today. But when someone asked how he was doing, they would finally have a real answer.
