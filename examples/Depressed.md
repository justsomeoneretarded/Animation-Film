# Depressed

A 3 minute 30 second short. One visible adult protagonist.

The female version uses the same story with she/her pronouns.

## 1. Bedroom — night

At two seventeen, he was still awake. His thumb kept moving, although nothing on the screen interested him anymore. He had been waiting to feel tired enough to sleep. Somehow, he was exhausted and wide awake at the same time.

## 2. Bedroom — night

The room was not a disaster. That almost made it harder to explain. There were things he could do, small ordinary things. Answer a message. Wash a cup. Each one seemed to require something he could no longer find.

## 3. Desk — night

At his desk, he tried the thing that usually helped: a list. He wrote the first line, then held the pen above the second. The page stayed mostly empty. He stared at it until it began to feel like evidence against him.

## 4. Desk — night

He had called himself lazy so often that the word arrived before any other thought. But he missed wanting things. He missed laughing without having to prepare for it. This was not a life he had chosen because it was easy.

## 5. Desk — dawn

When the window began to lighten, he turned to a clean page. This time, he wrote only two words: go outside. Underneath, he added a condition. He was allowed to come straight back. The promise felt small enough to believe.

## 6. Street — dawn

Outside, he stopped almost immediately. The air was colder than he expected, and the street seemed strangely far from his room. Part of him wanted to turn around before this became another thing he had tried and failed to do.

## 7. Street — dawn

He did not suddenly feel better. He walked past one doorway, then another, watching his shoes meet the pavement. He wasn't trying to become a different person before breakfast. For now, he was only walking to the end of the street.

## 8. Park — dawn

At the park, he sat down. His chest still felt heavy. For a moment, he was disappointed; some quiet part of him had hoped the walk would fix more than a walk could fix. He let his hands rest on his knees.

## 9. Park — day

The bench was cool through his clothes. A leaf scraped across the path, caught, and moved again. He noticed his breathing without trying to improve it. Nothing remarkable happened. But for a little while, he stopped measuring whether he was doing enough.

## 10. Park — day

When he took out his phone, he opened a message instead of the feed. He typed that he had been struggling, and that he didn't want to keep pretending otherwise. His thumb hovered for a long time. Then he sent it.

## 11. Desk — day

Back home, the unfinished list was exactly where he had left it. He did not fill the page. Beneath the two words, he wrote a different next step: arrange an appointment. He could ask for help before he knew how to explain everything.

## 12. Rooftop — day

Later, above the waking city, he stood without asking himself to feel grateful or strong. Tomorrow might still be difficult. He had not solved his life in a morning. But someone knew he was struggling now. He wouldn't have to hide all of it.
